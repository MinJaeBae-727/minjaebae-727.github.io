Research Focus

My research asks how plants remodel chromatin to survive cold. I study HOS15, the WD40-repeat substrate receptor of the CULLIN4–DDB1 E3 ubiquitin ligase (CRL4HOS15), and its chromatin substrates, the histone deacetylase HD2C and the linker histone H1.3, in Arabidopsis thaliana.

Linker histone H1.3 and chromatin decondensation in the cold

I found that HOS15 interacts preferentially with H1.3 among the three Arabidopsis H1 variants, that cold lowers H1 protein in a HOS15- and proteasome-dependent manner, and that H1.3 is the variant that is lost. h1.3 mutants are more freezing tolerant and suppress the freezing sensitivity of hos15-2. Their chromocenters are fewer and less condensed, resembling cold-treated wild-type nuclei, and NicE-seq shows increased accessibility mainly in pericentromeric heterochromatin. RNA-seq shows that the core CBF–COR program is intact in h1.3, whereas defense- and lignin-related genes are pre-activated and heat-shock and antioxidant genes are induced by cold only in h1.3.

HOS15 dimerization and CRL4HOS15 activity

I showed that HOS15 forms homodimers through its N-terminal LisH motif. Alanine substitutions of conserved hydrophobic LisH residues abolish dimerization in yeast, and these variants fail to support cold-induced ubiquitination and degradation of HD2C or to restore the freezing tolerance of hos15-2. I also characterize the intrinsically disordered region (IDR) within the HOS15 coiled-coil domain, and use LC-MS-based proteomics to profile proteins that change in hos15 and h1 mutants.
